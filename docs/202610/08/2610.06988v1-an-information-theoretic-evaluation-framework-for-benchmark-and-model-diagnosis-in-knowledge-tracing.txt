## Abstract 

Knowledge tracing (KT) models are predominantly evaluated using aggregate metrics such as area under the curve (AUC) and accuracy. However, these global scores obscure where the remaining errors originate and fail to indicate whether a benchmark is approaching saturation. While estimating a global theoretical perfor-mance limit is challenging in realistic KT settings, it is possible to quantify local predictability. To address this, we propose an information-theoretic evaluation framework for KT benchmark diagnosis. We use Context Tree Weighting (CTW) on item-response histories and current-item queries as an operational causal uncer-tainty coordinate, while distinguishing it from the unobserved Local Irreducible Uncertainty (LIU) under the full KT information set. By projecting predictions onto this shared uncertainty coordinate, we evaluate model performance gains across distinct entropy bands rather than only at the global level. Comprehensive evaluations on NIPS Task 3/4 and Algebra 2005 reveal that model improvements are highly non-uniform. Modern KT models show substantial gains in high-entropy regions, and additional item-aware references, log-loss, and equal-frequency anal-yses support this localization. The framework also flags regions where apparent gains require checks for noise-sensitive behavior. By surfacing these local mod-eling failures alongside genuine gains, this approach provides a diagnostic tool for studying both residual predictive structure and the limitations of current KT benchmarks and models. 

## 1 Introduction 

Knowledge tracing (KT) predicts a student’s future responses from their past interaction history, serving as a foundational task in artificial intelligence (AI) for education. As modern KT architectures grow increasingly sophisticated, performance on standard benchmarks continues to climb. However, the evaluation paradigm remains dominated by aggregate metrics such as area under the curve (AUC) and accuracy. These global scores are useful for leaderboard rankings, but they fail to answer two diagnostic questions that matter for progress: where does meaningful benchmark headroom remain? and what kind of errors is a model reducing? A marginal +0 .01 boost in global AUC cannot distinguish between a model that refines already-easy cases, one that extracts genuine structure from difficult interactions, and one whose apparent gain is concentrated in unstable regions where outcomes are close to random under the observable protocol. 

> Preprint. arXiv:2610.06988v1 [cs.LG] 4 Oct 2026

To address this gap, we propose an information-theoretic evaluation framework for KT benchmark and model diagnosis. The framework separates two roles that aggregate evaluation conflates. First, 

uncertainty characterization establishes a shared local coordinate for prediction difficulty. Because the true Local Irreducible Uncertainty (LIU) is not directly observable in finite KT data, we use Context Tree Weighting (CTW) as an operational causal anchor: it assigns each prediction point a fold-consistent entropy estimate using past item-response history and the current queried item. Second, predictive realization measures how much task-facing KT models and anchor-side neural probes improve on that coordinate. This decoupling lets us ask not only whether a benchmark is moving on average, but also whether gains occur in low-uncertainty regions, high-uncertainty regions with exploitable structure, or high-uncertainty regions that call for item or data inspection. Our contributions are as follows: (i) We introduce an information-theoretic evaluation paradigm that shifts KT assessment from global leaderboard scores to localized benchmark and model diagnosis; (ii) We formalize the separation between local irreducible uncertainty and realized predictive performance, with CTW serving as an operational causal anchor rather than as the ground-truth LIU; (iii) We provide a fold-consistent empirical protocol based on CTW-anchored entropy bands, gain-over-anchor curves, and item-level remaining-uncertainty/information-gain diagnostics; and (iv) Through broad aligned experiments on NIPS Task 3/4 and Algebra 2005, we show that the largest reliable gains occur in high-entropy regions while also identifying high-uncertainty, low-information-gain cases that warrant further reliability checks, with item-aware anchor and proper-score analyses testing the robustness of the localization. 

## 2 Related Work 

KT has progressed from Bayesian KT [Corbett and Anderson, 1994] to neural sequence models such as DKT [Piech et al., 2015], memory-based models such as DKVMN [Zhang et al., 2017], attention-based variants such as SAKT and AKT [Pandey and Karypis, 2019, Ghosh et al., 2020], and strong modern baselines such as Deep-IRT and simpleKT [Yeung, 2019, Liu et al., 2023b]. Toolkits and benchmarks including ASSISTments, EdNet, the NeurIPS 2020 Education Challenge, and pyKT have improved reproducibility [Heffernan and Heffernan, 2014, Choi et al., 2020b, Wang et al., 2021b, Liu et al., 2022]. However, these comparisons still primarily report aggregate metrics such as AUC and accuracy. Our work is closer to probabilistic forecast evaluation. Information-theoretic uncertainty [Cover and Thomas, 2006], universal coding tools such as CTW [Willems et al., 1995], and proper scoring rules such as Brier score and log-loss [Brier, 1950, Gneiting and Raftery, 2007] provide tools for separating uncertainty from realized predictive quality. We use CTW as a common causal anchor and evaluate model gains along entropy bands rather than only at the benchmark level. This makes the contribution diagnostic rather than architectural: the objective is to determine where benchmark progress is reliable, where model capacity changes predictions, and where apparent gains are more consistent with unstable or noise-like item behavior. 

## 3 Framework 

3.1 Problem setup and local uncertainty 

In standard KT, we observe a student’s interaction history up to time t, denoted as Xt =

{(q1, r 1), . . . , (qt, r t)}, where qk represents the practiced item and rk ∈ { 0, 1} represents the cor-rectness of the response. Given the next item qt+1 , the task is to predict the probability of a correct response: 

ˆpt+1 = ˆP (Rt+1 = 1 | Xt, q t+1 ). (1) To formalize the information available at this prediction step, we define the observable filtration as 

Ft = σ(Xt, q t+1 ). Based on this, we define the local irreducible uncertainty (LIU) of the prediction point as the conditional entropy of the true response Rt+1 :

LIU( Xt, q t+1 ) = H(P (Rt+1 | Xt, q t+1 )) . (2) This term represents the theoretical entropy remaining under the admissible KT information boundary, i.e., the inherent unpredictability even if we had a perfect model for the student’s historical data. A global score can decrease either because a model reduces epistemic error in a meaningful region or because gains and failures cancel across regions with very different local uncertainty. All entropies and KL divergences in the theoretical identities use base-2 logarithms, so they are measured in bits. The outer expectation in Fact 1 averages over prediction contexts; the local entropy is the entropy of Pt at a given context. To study uncertainty operationally, we use Context Tree Weighting (CTW) as an autoregressive causal anchor. In the real-data implementation, its context contains past item-response tokens, encoded as I:item|R:resp , and a query token Q:item for the current item. At prediction step 

t + 1 , these tokens are constructed from Xt and qt+1 ; the current response is observed only after prediction, and future responses are excluded. Thus item identity is a predictor input, while an explicit Q-matrix or skill-tag representation is not required. The corresponding uncertainty coordinate is ˆuctw ,t = H(pctw ). This is a model-dependent estimate under the causal information boundary 

Ft = σ(Xt, q t+1 ), not a response-only coordinate or a certified Bayes limit. Gains over CTW 3can reflect limitations of its finite-depth context model even when predictors share this information boundary. We also test localization under an independent ItemRasch reference that models item difficulty and online ability differently (Appendix E). Furthermore, we introduce a fused CTW+NN predictor. This model augments the pure CTW anchor by applying a neural refinement of the form pctw 7 → σ(logit( pctw ) + fϕ(Xt, q t+1 )) . In our evaluation protocol, the pure CTW model defines the shared uncertainty axis (i.e., the buckets), while the CTW+NN model acts as an anchor-side neural probe. It shows whether exploitable structural signals still exist beyond the capabilities of the symbolic source model. 

3.3 Operational bands, remaining uncertainty, and information gain 

Given an aligned evaluation set, our empirical protocol proceeds as follows: First, we compute a fold-consistent CTW prediction pctw ,i for each evaluation point i. Second, we convert this probability into an estimated entropy score ˆuctw ,i = H(pctw ,i ). Third, we partition the samples into distinct uncertainty buckets (using fixed-width bands in the original analysis and equal-frequency quintiles in the additional robustness analysis). Finally, we report both the absolute performance and the performance gain over the CTW anchor within each bucket. This resulting band view is deliberately model-agnostic: any KT baseline can be evaluated on this exact same CTW-defined coordinate. The same autoregressive anchor also enables fine-grained, item-level diagnostics. For any given item

> q

denote its average CTW conditional entropy across all predictions for that item. We define the remaining uncertainty

. A high RU identifies items whose outcomes remain unstable under the current observable protocol (e.g., potentially due to guessing, ambiguous wording, missing observables, or overly coarse step granularity); a high IG 

highlights items with lower anchor entropy than item-marginal entropy. This plug-in difference is a diagnostic rather than a guaranteed estimate of mutual information; anchor misspecification can affect either quantity. 

3.4 Benchmark and model diagnosis criteria 

The framework answers three diagnostic questions. First, benchmark diagnosis asks whether high-uncertainty regions with substantial support still admit reliable gains over the causal anchor; if strong models remain separated there, aggregate saturation claims are premature. Second, model diagnosis 

asks where a model improves and whether those improvements are supported by proper scoring behavior, not only by threshold accuracy. Third, noise-sensitive behavior asks whether apparent improvements occur in high-RU/low-IG regions where the item-conditioned anchor leaves substantial uncertainty. Such movements should not be interpreted as progress unless they are stable across folds and accompanied by proper-score improvements. 

## 4 Experimental Protocol 

We evaluate five-fold aligned exports on NIPS Task 3/4 and Algebra 2005. ASSIST2015 is reported as a global auxiliary check, while the local diagnostic figures focus on NIPS Task 3/4 and Algebra 2005 because these datasets support the band-level and item-level diagnostics used in the main figures. Each prediction point is assigned a CTW entropy value and placed into one of five entropy bands [0 .0, 0.2) , [0 .2, 0.4) , [0 .4, 0.6) , [0 .6, 0.8) , [0 .8, 1.0] . We report AUC, accuracy, and Brier score globally and within bands. Accuracy gives the decision-level view, while Brier score is used as a proper-score check on whether probability quality improves in the same region. The model pool includes the CTW anchor, the CTW+NN neural probe, and a broad KT sweep spanning DKT, DKT+, DKT-forget, KQN, SAKT, SAINT, AKT, simpleKT, sparseKT, FoLiBiKT, stableKT, extraKT, Deep-IRT, ATKT, ATKTFix, DIMKT, ATDKT, and HawkesKT [Yeung and Yeung, 2018, Nagatani et al., 2019, Lee and Yeung, 2019, Choi et al., 2020a, Guo et al., 2021, Huang et al., 2023, Im et al., 2023, Li et al., 2024a,b, Shen et al., 2022, Liu et al., 2023a, Wang et al., 2021a]. The main bucket coordinate is always computed from CTW alone, so task-facing KT baselines do not define the axis on which they are judged. Model diagnosis is then based on the alignment between gain-over-anchor curves, proper-score changes, fold consistency, and item-level RU/IG signals. ## 5 Results 

5.1 Sanity check: same marginal correctness, different causal uncertainty 

Before real benchmarks, we verify the property needed from the anchor. We construct two binary response regimes with nearly identical marginal correctness rates. The first is an i.i.d. Bernoulli (0 .5) 

process. The second is a symmetric persistent two-state Markov process: the initial state is uniform over {0, 1}, and each subsequent response copies the previous response with probability 0.95 and flips with probability 0.05 . This process has stationary marginal correctness 0.5 but forms long correct/incorrect runs. Across 64 sequences of length 400 , the observed correctness rates are 0.492 

and 0.495 , yet average CTW entropy is 0.986 bits for the i.i.d. regime and 0.318 bits for the persistent regime. CTW predictive accuracy is 0.501 versus 0.945 .

5.2 Global aligned performance is insufficient for diagnosis 

Global aligned performance is used as context rather than as the main diagnostic target. Across the aligned folds, CTW+NN improves the CTW anchor on all three datasets while remaining an anchor-side neural probe rather than the definition of the uncertainty axis: +0 .0823 AUC and +0 .0690 

accuracy on NIPS Task 3/4, +0 .0835 AUC and +0 .0160 accuracy on Algebra 2005, and +0 .0455 

AUC and +0 .0142 accuracy on ASSIST2015. The task-facing KT sweep is reported in Table 1. These numbers show that stronger predictors improve on average, but they do not reveal whether the improvement comes from already predictable regions, genuinely difficult but structured regions, or unstable regions where apparent gains require caution. The central claim is local: global scores provide a sanity check, while the entropy-conditioned bands below test where benchmark headroom and model limitations actually lie. 

5.3 Benchmark diagnosis through entropy bands 

The main empirical pattern is local rather than global. On NIPS Task 3/4, the accuracy improvement of CTW+NN over CTW grows from +0 .0026 in the lowest entropy band to +0 .0886 in the highest. On Algebra 2005, gains are small in lower bands but reach +0 .0283 accuracy and 0.0681 Brier reduction in the highest band. Moreover, the highest band is not a tiny tail: it contains roughly 74 .8% 

of NIPS Task 3/4 samples and 55 .6% of Algebra 2005 samples in the shared aligned subsets. These high-entropy regions are therefore central to the benchmark rather than peripheral outliers.

This is exactly the regime where a local diagnostic is more informative than a global score: aggregate performance averages together easy regions that are already largely solved and high-uncertainty regions where model choice still matters. This interpretation also clarifies why CTW+NN is included. It is not used to define the benchmark axis, and it is not presented as a new KT leaderboard method. Its role is to act as an anchor-side neural probe: if a simple neural refinement of the causal anchor improves most in high-entropy regions, then those regions should not be dismissed as irreducible noise. They are hard, but not exhausted. By contrast, flat delta curves in high-entropy bands across strong models would indicate a more saturated benchmark under the same observable protocol. The same profile is also a model-diagnosis tool, but the roles must remain separate: CTW+NN is an anchor-side probe for remaining signal, not a task-facing KT baseline. For task-facing models, a gain is most credible when threshold accuracy and Brier score move in the same direction within the same region. Isolated accuracy changes in high-entropy regions should be inspected rather than immediately counted as progress, because high entropy can arise from both exploitable hidden structure and effectively random variation under the observed features. This is why the framework pairs band-level gains with item-level RU/IG diagnostics: the band profile identifies where a model changes behavior, while RU/IG asks whether that region contains contextual signal or unstable item behavior.

Second, item-level RU and IG separate item regions whose outcomes remain unstable from regions where history carries strong predictive signal. This is the main mechanism by which the framework surfaces noise-sensitive model behavior. High-RU/low-IG items motivate inspection for ambiguity, guessing, missing observables, or problematic granularity; if a model’s apparent improvement is concentrated in such items without proper-score or fold-stability support, the improvement is a warning signal for unstable prediction behavior; it does not by itself establish that the model has learned random noise. The RU/IG view makes the distinction concrete. High-RU/low-IG regions have high anchor entropy relative to item-marginal entropy, so apparent model gains on them require proper-score and fold-stability checks before being treated as progress. ## 6 Discussion and Limitations 

The results support information-theoretic reporting as a benchmark- and model-diagnosis tool. A benchmark close to saturation should show tight clustering of strong models especially in high-uncertainty regions. We do not observe that pattern for NIPS Task 3/4 or Algebra 2005: the hardest CTW-defined regions still contain the largest gains. This suggests that aggregate KT scores hide substantial local structure and that residual structure remains under the tested references and evaluation protocols; it does not quantify distance to the Bayes limit. 9CTW is used here as a causal anchor and common analysis coordinate, not as a complete model of student learning. This localizes where headroom remains while avoiding the confound that would arise if every model defined its own uncertainty axis. The refined-coordinate and Brier checks reduce the risk that the conclusion is an artifact of CTW-only bucketization or threshold accuracy. At the same time, the framework avoids treating every high-entropy gain as progress: high-RU/low-IG items expose regions where model changes should be interpreted as noise-sensitive unless supported by proper-score improvement and fold stability. The analysis has several scope boundaries. The operational coordinates are model-dependent es-timates, and their entropy is not a certified lower bound on achievable error. Five-fold intervals describe variation across the retained folds; overlapping training sets limit interpretations based on independent-fold assumptions. The additional CTW+NN-SKT analysis uses a distinct common cohort and must not be numerically pooled with the original reported branch. First, alternative uncertainty coordinates may reveal complementary structure. Second, some raw KT exports have dataset-specific coverage differences, so the main claims emphasize shared aligned subsets. Third, Figure 6 reports model diagnosis at the RU/IG-region level, while the paper reports item-level RU/IG summaries for NIPS Task 3/4 and Algebra 2005. 

## 7 Conclusion 

We introduced an information-theoretic evaluation framework for KT benchmark and model diagnosis. By separating uncertainty characterization from predictive realization and using CTW entropy as a shared causal coordinate, the framework shows where model progress occurs. Across broad aligned sweeps, the largest gains concentrate in high-uncertainty regions, demonstrating that these benchmarks contain substantial exploitable structure beyond what aggregate scores reveal. The practical message is that KT benchmarks should not be judged only by aggregate score movement. A small global improvement can be meaningful if it occurs in a difficult, high-uncertainty region with substantial support and proper-score improvement; conversely, gains concentrated in high-RU/low-IG regions may reflect noise-sensitive behavior. A minimal adoption path is straightforward: report global metrics, add entropy-conditioned gain curves on a fixed causal coordinate, and inspect RU/IG regions when the curve shows remaining headroom.

B.2 Preprocessing protocol and alignment rules 

The main benchmark-diagnosis figures use question-level preprocessing for NIPS Task 3/4 and Algebra 2005. ASSIST2015 is reported as an auxiliary consistency check under the convention

B.3 Knowledge-component mapping and Q-matrix status 

The CTW anchor itself does not require a Q-matrix. It uses historical item-response tokens and the next queried item’s token as predictor inputs, in addition to using item identifiers for prediction alignment and item-level diagnostics. Prediction precedes the current-response update. The appendix does not introduce an additional inferred KC mapping layer.

C.3 Compute-cost discussion 

The CTW anchor is intentionally lightweight. For a fixed depth D, each prediction/update step touches only the suffix path of length at most D, so the per-step symbolic overhead is linear in D

and small in practice. In our experiments we use D = 6 , which is sufficient to separate the i.i.d. and persistent synthetic regimes while keeping the causal anchor inexpensive. This is one of the main practical advantages of the framework: the uncertainty analysis adds a modest symbolic pass on top of predictions that are already being generated for KT evaluation, whereas the expensive part of the pipeline remains the training of the task-facing neural KT models.

E.7 Single-probe architecture and training protocol 

CTW+NN-SKT adds a SimpleKT residual to the CTW logit. The strict checkpoints use 2 or 4 SimpleKT blocks, four attention heads, and feed-forward width 256. Model selection uses validation negative log-likelihood, with at most 50 epochs and early-stopping patience 4. NIPS settings use hidden width 64 or 128, dropout 0.10 or 0.20, learning rate 5 × 10 −4 or 10 −3, and batch size 64 or 128. Algebra settings use hidden width 128 or 256, dropout 0.05, 0.10, or 0.20, learning rate 10 −4,and batch size 64 or 128. These reported ranges document the additional branch but do not identify every selected dataset-fold configuration. Complete selected configurations, seeds, best epochs, validation NLL values, and optimizer metadata are required for exact retraining; they are not available in the provided archive. The archived aggregate results and the reported additional comparisons support the diagnostic analysis, while exact retraining remains a reproducibility limitation.

## Responsible use and writing assistance 

This study analyzes existing educational benchmarks and does not collect new human-subject data. The diagnostic scores should guide benchmark inspection and model evaluation; they should not be used alone to make decisions about individual students. High anchor entropy can reflect missing variables or model mismatch rather than an intrinsic limitation of a learner. Data access and reuse remain subject to each original provider’s terms.
