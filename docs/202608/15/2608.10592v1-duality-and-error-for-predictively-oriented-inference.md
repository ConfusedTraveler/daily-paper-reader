## 摘要
面向预测（PrO）推断通过选择模型参数上的分布来量化不确定性，该分布优化应用于所诱导预测分布的评分规则，并加上相对于参考分布的散度惩罚。通过对模型密度取平均后应用评分规则，PrO推断以预测性能为目标，并考虑模型误设定。我们关注对数评分与一般φ-散度正则化。我们的贡献有两方面。首先，我们推导了PrO推断的有限维对偶形式。对于n个观测，对偶问题有n+1个变量。我们建立了零对偶间隙准则以及联系原始问题与对偶问题解的最优性条件。当原始问题和对偶问题的解存在时，这些条件给出PrO后验的半解析表示，以及用于评估数值解精度的证书。对于Kullback--Leibler正则化，后验具有指数形式。其次，我们推导了近似PrO后验的有限样本超额预测风险界，该界将采样波动、散度预算下的逼近、正则化和数值优化误差分开。该结果甚至适用于基准预测风险无法由任何与参考分布具有有限散度的模型参数上的概率分布达到的情况。我们使用一个可精确求解的分类示例表明，预测风险收敛可以蕴含收敛到唯一预测分布，即使参数分布在原始参数空间上没有弱极限。该示例还表明，不同的φ-散度可能需要不同的正则化调度。最后，我们以一个误设定的高斯位置混合示例来说明对偶计算、原始解恢复和数值精度检查。

## Abstract
Predictively oriented (PrO) inference quantifies uncertainty by selecting a distribution over model parameters to optimize a scoring rule applied to the induced predictive distribution, together with a divergence penalty from a reference distribution. By applying the scoring rule after averaging model densities, PrO inference targets predictive performance, accounting for model misspecification. We focus on the logarithmic score with general $φ$-divergence regularization. Our contributions are twofold. First, we derive a finite-dimensional dual formulation of PrO inference. For $n$ observations, the dual problem has $n+1$ variables. We establish zero-duality-gap criteria and optimality conditions that relate the primal and dual solutions. When primal and dual solutions exist, these conditions yield a semi-analytical representation of the PrO posterior and certificates for assessing the accuracy of numerical solutions. For Kullback--Leibler regularization, the posterior has an exponential form. Second, we derive a finite-sample excess predictive-risk bound for approximate PrO posteriors that separates sampling fluctuation, approximation under a divergence budget, regularization, and numerical optimization error. The result applies even when the benchmark predictive risk is not attained by any probability distribution over the model parameters having finite divergence from the reference distribution. We use an exactly solvable categorical example to show that predictive-risk convergence can imply convergence to a unique predictive distribution even though the parameter distributions have no weak limit on the original parameter space. The example also shows that different $φ$-divergences can require different regularization schedules. We conclude with a misspecified Gaussian location-mixture example that illustrates the dual computation, primal recovery, and numerical accuracy checks.
