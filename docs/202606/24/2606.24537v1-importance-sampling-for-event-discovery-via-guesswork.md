## 摘要
传统重要性采样（IS）旨在通过最小化估计方差来估计罕见事件概率。然而，许多应用优先考虑快速发现：生成一条进入罕见集$A_n$的轨迹。这需要从基于集合的估计转向以击中时间$τ_{A_n} := \inf\{t \ge 1 : Y_t^n \in A_n\}$为中心的设计原则。我们将发现问题形式化为在名义模型$p$下最小化发现轨迹的描述长度（惊奇度）的问题。我们证明，最小化该描述长度等价于最小化名义秩指数$J_{\mathrm{rank}}(q_n) := \lim_{n\to\infty} \frac{1}{n} \log G_n(Y^n)$，其中$G_n(x^n)$是序列$x^n$的猜测度。对于独立同分布模型和类型定义的罕见集$Γ$，我们表明，经典IS针对质量主导类型$Q_{\mathrm{IS}}^* \in \arg\min_{Q \in Γ} D(Q\|p)$，而发现最优性由$Q_{\mathrm{GW}}^* \in \arg\min_{Q \in Γ} [H(Q) + D(Q\|p)]$实现。该框架确定了一个基本规则：最小化猜测度指数确保发现的序列是相对于名义模型搜索顺序而言集合中“最不令人惊讶”的代表。我们进一步证明，在预算约束下，当击中时间最小化不唯一时，该指数作为字典序的打破平局者。这确立了$H(Q) + D(Q\|p)$作为基于发现的重要性采样的自然目标，在随机采样与系统搜索之间建立了正式桥梁。

## Abstract
Traditional importance sampling (IS) is designed to estimate rare-event probabilities by minimizing estimator variance. However, many applications prioritize rapid discovery: the generation of a trajectory within a rare set $A_n$. This requires a shift from ensemble-based estimation to a design principle focused on the hitting time $τ_{A_n} := \inf\{t \ge 1 : Y_t^n \in A_n\}$.   We formalize a Quality of Discovery problem as the problem of minimizing the description length (surprisal) of the discovered trajectory under the nominal model $p$. We prove that minimizing this description length is equivalent to minimizing the nominal rank exponent $J_{\mathrm{rank}}(q_n) := \lim_{n\to\infty} \frac{1}{n} \log G_n(Y^n)$, where $G_n(x^n)$ is the guesswork of sequence $x^n$. For i.i.d.\ models and type-defined rare sets $Γ$, we show that while classical IS targets the mass-dominating type $Q_{\mathrm{IS}}^* \in \arg\min_{Q \in Γ} D(Q\|p)$, discovery optimality is achieved by $Q_{\mathrm{GW}}^* \in \arg\min_{Q \in Γ} [H(Q) + D(Q\|p)]$. This framework identifies a fundamental rule: minimizing the guesswork exponent ensures the discovered sequence is the "least surprising" representative of the set relative to the nominal model's search order. We further demonstrate that under budgetary constraints, this exponent serves as a lexicographic tie-breaker when the hitting-time minimizer is not unique. This establishes $H(Q) + D(Q\|p)$ as a natural objective for discovery-based importance sampling, providing a formal bridge between randomized sampling and systematic search.
