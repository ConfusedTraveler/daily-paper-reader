## 摘要
自适应共形推断（ACI）通过根据近期覆盖误差在线调整名义未覆盖水平，将共形预测扩展到非可交换情形。然而，当预测以预测视界 $τ$ 发布时，评估一个预测所需的结果仅在 $τ$ 步之后才能观测到，因此这些自适应更新必须依赖延迟反馈。我们研究这一设定，并考察延迟的影响如何取决于残差过程的持续性。首先，我们表明，$τ$-延迟 ACI 递推可以分解为 $τ$ 个交织的类 ACI 序列。这一表示给出了长期经验覆盖率的有限样本界，并显式依赖于 $τ$。我们还推导了一个近似边缘覆盖界，将覆盖偏差与预测视界内底层环境的变化以及自适应速率 $γ$ 联系起来。随后，我们引入延迟-记忆比 $r=τ/L$，其中 $L$ 是驱动非可交换性的时间信号衰减的时间尺度。模拟结果表明，该比率的有效性取决于时间依赖的形式：在 AR(1) 依赖下，它能有力地组织性能表现；在 GARCH(1,1) 和马尔可夫切换下，它对总体性能的预测性较弱，但更清晰地刻画了在这些设定中尺度归一化何时仍然有用。均值突变和方差突变实验进一步表明，偏好的自适应速率取决于残差动态。总体而言，结果表明，预测延迟的影响最好相对于过去残差信息仍然相关的时间尺度来理解。

## Abstract
Adaptive Conformal Inference (ACI) extends conformal prediction to non-exchangeable settings by adjusting the nominal miscoverage level online in response to recent coverage errors. When forecasts are issued with horizon $τ$, however, the outcome needed to evaluate a prediction is observed only $τ$ steps later, so these adaptive updates must rely on delayed feedback. We study this setting and ask how the effect of delay depends on the persistence of the residual process. First, we show that the $τ$-delayed ACI recursion can be decomposed into $τ$ interleaved ACI-like sequences. This representation yields a finite-sample bound on long-run empirical coverage with explicit dependence on $τ$. We also derive an approximate marginal coverage bound that relates coverage deviation to changes in the underlying environment across the forecast horizon and to the adaptation rate $γ$. We then introduce the delay-to-memory ratio $r=τ/L$, where $L$ is the time scale over which the temporal signal driving non-exchangeability decays. Simulation results show that the usefulness of this ratio depends on the form of temporal dependence: it strongly organizes performance under AR(1) dependence, is less predictive of overall performance under GARCH(1,1) and Markov switching, but more clearly characterizes when scale normalization remains useful in those settings. Abrupt mean- and variance-shift experiments further show that the preferred adaptation rate depends on the residual dynamics. Overall, the results show that the effect of forecast delay is best understood relative to the time scale over which past residual information remains relevant.
