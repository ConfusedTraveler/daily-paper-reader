## ABSTRACT 

Multivariate volatility forecasting across international equity markets presents a fundamental information-set problem: asynchronous exchange closures dictate which market observations strictly belong to the information filtration at any given forecast origin. Rather than asking whether an unconstrained neural network can achieve superior in-sample fit, this paper investigates whether carefully regularized, origin-admissible cross-market information delivers statistically significant incremental forecast accuracy beyond established volatility models. We develop PGA-Trans-HAR, a parsimonious forecasting system that combines a forecast-origin-admissible ridge-VAR/GFEVD predictive-connectedness prior with data-driven spatial self-attention. A learned, time-invariant market-specific gate governs their relative allocation, asymmetric attention masking prevents closed exchanges from transmitting spurious zero-volatility signals, and a frozen direct-horizon HAR baseline anchors a bounded neural residual correction in the inverse-softplus domain. Using high-frequency data from eight major global stock indices over the 2006–2022 period, we evaluate direct forecasts at horizons of 1, 5, and 22 union-calendar days across all-days and common-days panels, five-seed ensembles, structural ablations, HAC-adjusted Diebold–Mariano tests, and block-bootstrap Model Confidence Sets. Relative to the univariate HAR benchmark, the constrained framework reduces both MSE and MAE across all eight markets at the daily and weekly horizons, and across seven of the eight markets at the monthly horizon. Within the broader suite of linear and deep learning benchmarks, it achieves the lowest cross-market average MAE at the daily horizon (while linear HAR-KS minimizes daily MSE), and the lowest average MSE and MAE at the weekly and monthly horizons. Structural ablations demonstrate that architectural flexibility is not monotonically beneficial: the spatial allocation restriction is essential, with learned market-specific gates improving forecast accuracy over a uniform equal-weight mixture at weekly and monthly horizons, whereas daily forecasts benefit from stronger scalar shrinkage. The empirical evidence supports a qualified affirmative conclusion: disciplined, origin-aligned cross-market information yields genuine incremen-tal predictive value over the HAR anchor, with the most pronounced gains emerging at medium and long horizons where structural spillovers persist.

## 1 Introduction 

Forecasting global realized volatility is central to financial risk management, portfolio allocation, and derivative pricing (Andersen et al., 2003). As international financial markets have grown increasingly interdependent, cross-market volatility spillovers have become highly informative for forecasting domestic risk (Diebold and Yilmaz, 2012). However, forecasting multivariate volatility across global markets introduces a fundamental information-set problem: the presence of asynchronous trading calendars. Because international exchanges operate on disparate holiday schedules, global volatility panels are inherently misaligned. Traditional econometric approaches typically handle these asynchronous closures by either truncating non-common trading days—which distorts the temporal continuity of the time series—or by artificially padding missing entries with zeros, which injects uninformative noise into the estimation process (Burns et al., 1998). This misalignment severely complicates multi-horizon out-of-sample forecasting, as models risk inadvertently incorporating future information if the predictive information set is not strictly bounded at the forecast origin. Figure 1 makes this measurement problem concrete. The Christmas interval contains dates on which some markets report valid volatility while others are closed. N225, for example, is active on 25 December 2019 when the remaining seven exchanges are closed; SPX, N225, and KS11 are active on 26 December while several European and Asian exchanges remain closed. Retaining these dates preserves information from the open markets, while the mask prevents absent observations from being interpreted as unusually low volatility. To capture complex, non-linear volatility spillovers, recent literature has increasingly adopted highly parameterized machine learning architectures, such as Spatio-Temporal Graph Neural Networks (STGNNs) and Transformer-based models (Zhang et al., 2025). While these models offer immense representational flexibility, they introduce a distinct 2Forecasting Global Volatility Across Asynchronous Markets: Incremental Accuracy from Constrained Cross-Market Attention A P REPRINT 

tension between complexity and parsimony. Financial volatility time series are characterized by a notably low signal-to-noise ratio. In such environments, purely data-driven neural networks are highly susceptible to overfitting transient transactional noise rather than learning robust economic linkages. Furthermore, existing graphical forecasting frameworks often construct relational networks using static, full-sample covariance matrices. This practice violates strict out-of-sample information boundaries and obscures whether the reported forecasting gains stem from genuine predictive transmission or from implicit look-ahead bias. This paper proposes a fundamentally different approach. We design a parsimonious forecasting system that does not attempt to replace classical econometric models with an unconstrained "black box," but rather utilizes the widely estab-lished Heterogeneous Autoregressive (HAR) model (Corsi, 2009) as a strong, frozen benchmark anchor. Consequently, the central empirical question of this study is not whether a complex neural architecture unconditionally outperforms linear models, but rather: Does carefully constrained, origin-admissible cross-market information produce statistically significant incremental forecast accuracy beyond established volatility models? To systematically address this question, we introduce a hybrid forecasting framework that tightly constraints a spatio-temporal attention mechanism utilizing economic priors. The architecture minimizes parameter explosion and safeguards out-of-sample integrity through four specific methodological contributions: 1. Forecast-origin-admissible information alignment. We formulate the daily international panel on a union calendar while distinguishing observed market-days from exchange closures. Both the neural window and the rolling prior are indexed by the forecast origin. The prior is refreshed once every 20 origins, rather than on future target dates, so the same information rule applies at h = 1 , 5, and 22 .2. A constrained cross-market information channel. We combine a rolling ridge-VAR/GFEVD prior with data-driven spatial attention. A single learned gate gn for each destination market captures persistent heterogeneity in the preferred allocation, while remaining fixed across observations, dates, source markets, and spatio-temporal blocks. The restriction avoids the additional estimation variation of an observation-dependent mixture. 3. Benchmark-centered nonlinear forecasting. A direct, node-specific HAR model is estimated separately for each horizon and frozen. The neural system contributes only a bounded residual in the inverse-softplus domain. Positivity is preserved, and the empirical content of the nonlinear branch is explicitly incremental to HAR. A node-balanced objective further limits the extent to which aggregate improvements can be purchased with a large relative deterioration in one market. 4. Evidence on when restricted cross-market information helps. We compare the system with HAR, multivariate HAR specifications, static graph models, a pure spatio-temporal Transformer, and nested structural ablations. Evaluation uses all-days and common-days panels, three forecast horizons, five-seed ensembles, HAC-adjusted Diebold–Mariano tests, and block-bootstrap Model Confidence Sets. This design separates incremental predictive content from a generic comparison between a deep model and a linear benchmark. The evidence is horizon- and loss-dependent. Relative to HAR, PGA-Trans-HAR lowers both MSE and MAE for every market at h = 1 and h = 5 , and for seven of eight markets at h = 22 . In the broader comparison, it has the lowest cross-market average daily MAE but not MSE, and the lowest average MSE and MAE at the two longer horizons. The predictive-ability tests and Model Confidence Sets remain heterogeneous across markets. The ablations also reject a simple complexity interpretation: temporal attention and the rolling graph are not unconditional complements, and the learned market-specific gate helps relative to a common g = 0 .5 at h = 5 and h = 22 but not at h = 1 . The results therefore support a qualified conclusion that carefully constrained cross-market information can improve upon HAR, particularly at longer horizons; they do not establish uniform model dominance or structural volatility transmission. The remainder of the paper reviews the relevant forecasting literatures, formalizes the sequence of information and capacity restrictions, describes the evaluation design, and reports the predictive and diagnostic evidence. 

## 2 Related Literature 

2.1 Volatility Forecasting and the HAR Baseline 

The Heterogeneous Autoregressive (HAR) model captures realized-volatility persistence through a parsimonious cascade of daily, weekly, and monthly components (Corsi, 2009). Rather than attempting to strictly estimate a fractional-integration process, the HAR framework is highly appealing because it provides a transparent, computationally stable approximation of long-memory volatility dynamics. Furthermore, empirical evidence demonstrating that the composition of realized variation significantly influences predictability over different time spans reinforces the standard practice of employing direct, horizon-specific forecasting (Patton and Sheppard, 2015). 3Forecasting Global Volatility Across Asynchronous Markets: Incremental Accuracy from Constrained Cross-Market Attention A P REPRINT 

Consequently, the HAR model serves as more than just a conventional benchmark in this study; it establishes the foundational baseline that any constrained cross-market information must demonstrably improve upon. While the standard univariate HAR deliberately omits foreign-market predictors, multivariate frameworks, such as the Vector HAR (VHAR) and unrestricted HAR-Kitchen Sink (HAR-KS) extensions, incorporate them via linear regressors. Evaluating these alternatives helps isolate two distinct empirical questions: first, whether other global markets contain genuine incremental predictive content; and second, whether a complex, non-linear representation is strictly necessary to extract it. Established evidence of international volatility transmission naturally motivates the first question, demonstrating that predictive relationships and spillover intensities vary significantly across different market pairs (Bubák et al., 2011). Therefore, the empirical comparison in this paper is fundamentally organized around quantifying the incremental accuracy relative to the HAR anchor, rather than endorsing model complexity for its own sake. 

2.2 Predictive Connectedness in Multivariate Systems 

The generalized forecast-error variance decomposition (GFEVD) developed by Pesaran and Shin (1998) yields order-invariant variance shares, which Diebold and Yilmaz (2012) leverage to quantify directional predictive connectedness. This methodology elegantly compresses high-dimensional multivariate histories into directed spillover allocations across source markets. In a forecasting context, this representation is highly valuable because it provides a parsimonious summary of where predictive variation originates within the estimated VAR system. Crucially, however, the GFEVD remains a reduced-form predictive tool rather than a structurally identified causal network. Recent graph-based forecasting architectures impose analogous restrictions to govern the flow of cross-market infor-mation across a panel. For instance, GNN-HAR and diffusion-recurrent specifications propagate volatility signals through a prespecified adjacency matrix (Zhang et al., 2025; Chi et al., 2026). The out-of-sample validity of these models, however, depends critically on how this matrix is estimated and temporally aligned. While a static graph derived from the full sample or a fixed pre-test period offers structural parsimony, it risks either injecting inadmissible future information (look-ahead bias) or enforcing stale relationships that fail to adapt to market regime shifts. Although a rolling graphical approach permits predictive connectedness to evolve, its estimation window and update calendar must strictly respect the predictive information set available at the forecast origin. Consequently, the forecasting system proposed in this paper explicitly utilizes a strictly origin-admissible rolling ridge-VAR/GFEVD graph as a regularizing prior, rather than treating it as a maintained description of structural spillovers. 

2.3 Machine Learning and the Need for Economic Constraints 

Self-attention mechanisms estimate temporal and cross-sectional dependencies directly from observed realizations without imposing predefined functional forms (Vaswani et al., 2017). This architectural flexibility is particularly valuable when cross-market linkages exhibit complex non-linearities or evolving transmission channels that cannot be adequately captured by static relational graphs. However, unconstrained flexibility is notoriously hazardous in financial forecasting: the underlying economic signal is weak relative to high-frequency transactional noise, and the number of observations is modest relative to the set of possible interactions (Bucci, 2020; Sezer et al., 2020). From a forecasting perspective, the appropriate methodological response is not to dismiss flexible machine learning architectures, but rather to restrict where and how their flexibility enters the predictive system ( ?). In our proposed framework, this economic regularization operates across multiple structural levels: ridge shrinkage in the rolling VAR stabilizes the econometric connectedness prior; a market-specific gate regulates the reliance on data-driven attention relative to that structural prior; and the final forecast remains anchored to HAR. The gate is constant over dates within a fitted model. It therefore captures persistent heterogeneity in predictive allocation, not a time-varying market state. By imposing these deliberate constraints, the empirical evaluation shifts away from whether an unconstrained end-to-end neural network can overfit historical volatility levels, focusing instead on whether constrained cross-market attention extracts genuine incremental predictive accuracy beyond established benchmarks. 

2.4 The Asynchronous Calendar Challenge 

International financial market observations are inherently asynchronous. Country-specific holidays generate calendar dates on which realized-volatility measures are available for some markets but missing for others, while differences in trading hours create a separate intraday timing problem (Burns et al., 1998). In a unified multi-market panel, listwise deletion of non-common dates discards economically valuable information from active exchanges and disrupts temporal persistence. Conversely, imputing market closures with artificial zeros confounds the physical absence of measurement with an erroneous state of near-zero volatility. Crucially, in direct multi-step forecasting, every feature constructed for a target dated t must be strictly observable at the forecast origin o = t − h. Asynchrony is therefore not merely a technical data-alignment issue, but a boundary condition on the forecast information set. 4Forecasting Global Volatility Across Asynchronous Markets: Incremental Accuracy from Constrained Cross-Market Attention A P REPRINT 

These information-set boundaries apply with equal force to estimated objects as they do to raw inputs. In particular, a trailing relational graph is econometrically admissible only if its historical estimation window terminates on or before the forecast origin, and its rolling update calendar must be indexed strictly by origin time rather than by the future forecast target. The asymmetric masking mechanism formulated below preserves valid open-market observations while preventing a closed market from supplying a nonexistent volatility signal. We acknowledge, however, that while this mask resolves calendar-level misalignments, it does not reconstruct intraday closing sequences or identify structural news propagation, both of which would require higher-frequency tick data. Finally, rigorous out-of-sample evaluation across asynchronous panels requires formal statistical accounting for sampling uncertainty and multiple-model comparison. We implement pairwise Diebold–Mariano tests (Diebold and Mariano, 2002) equipped with heteroskedasticity-and-autocorrelation-consistent (HAC) long-run variance estimators (Newey and West, 1987, 1994) to evaluate overlapping multi-step loss differentials. These are complemented by the Model Confidence Set (MCS) procedure of Hansen et al. (2011), which identifies a subset of superior models for which equal predictive ability cannot be rejected at a prescribed confidence level. Importantly, neither procedure conflates a marginally lower sample average loss with conclusive evidence of universal predictive dominance, thereby ensuring a disciplined and realistic interpretation of cross-model comparisons. 

## 3 The Forecasting System 

The forecasting system is organized as a sequence of restrictions on the use of information. The calendar mask determines which observations may act as sources; the rolling connectedness matrix restricts the cross-market search; the fixed market gate limits how far data-driven attention may depart from that prior; and the frozen HAR forecast anchors the final prediction. This structure is deliberately parsimonious. Its purpose is to test whether disciplined cross-market information adds to a strong volatility benchmark, rather than to maximize neural flexibility. Figure 2 summarizes the system’s information flow. The upper path transforms the masked 22-position panel through two spatio-temporal blocks and produces a bounded neural residual. The lower path constructs mask-aware HAR features and supplies a frozen, horizon-specific positive anchor. The two paths meet only at the inverse-softplus fusion stage. This separation makes the source of each restriction transparent: the rolling prior and asymmetric source mask discipline the spatial channel, whereas the HAR path limits the magnitude of the final nonlinear departure.

## 4 Experimental Design 

The empirical design is built around three questions. First, does the proposed system add forecast accuracy beyond HAR and other established volatility models? Second, if it does, is the improvement associated with disciplined cross-market information rather than neural complexity alone? Third, are the rankings robust to evaluating only dates on which all markets trade? The split, benchmark, ablation, and inference procedures below are chosen to answer these questions without using test-period outcomes for model selection. 

4.1 Data Description and the Union Calendar 

The empirical dataset encompasses daily realized-volatility measures across eight major global equity indices: the S&P 500 (SPX), DAX (GDAXI), CAC 40 (FCHI), FTSE 100 (FTSE), OMX Stockholm (OMXSPI), Nikkei 225 (N225), KOSPI (KS11), and Hang Seng (HSI). Spanning October 24, 2006, through June 28, 2022, the union calendar comprises 

T = 4 ,079 trading dates on which at least one international exchange was active. To preserve the temporal continuity of each market and avoid discarding economically relevant observations from active exchanges, dates on which a specific exchange is closed remain in the panel as node-specific missing observations rather than being truncated. To guarantee strict data consistency and eliminate potential recording artifacts, the data-loading pipeline maps all nonpositive entries—including exchange-specific zero closure codes—to missing values ( NaN ) prior to constructing the binary activity mask. Numerical zeros are subsequently introduced strictly as computational placeholders during tensor formatting, where they are invariably paired with an inactive mask indicator ( mt,n = 0 ). Equation (2) formalizes the transformation logic governing scale consistency: specifying input_format=rv applies the 100 √· scaling exactly once, whereas input_format=sqrt_rv retains the supplied values without alteration. All empirical experiments reported herein utilize the pre-transformed df_union_sqrt.csv panel under input_format=sqrt_rv . Consequently, the loader applies no secondary square-root transformation or additional rescaling, ensuring that all out-of-sample loss metrics are evaluated directly on the percentage square-root realized-volatility scale. Figure 1 provides a representative view of the resulting activity mask and shows that valid open-market observations coexist with node-specific closures on the same union-calendar date. 

4.2 Strict Forecast-Origin Alignment Protocol 

The protocol separates model selection from final estimation. Hyperparameter selection uses only the 60–10 development split; the selected training length is then treated as fixed, and every parameter is re-estimated from a fresh initialization on the first 70%. For each horizon and random seed, estimation proceeds in three stages. 1. The model is trained on the first 60% and evaluated on the following 10%. AdamW uses an initial learning rate of 5 × 10 −4, cosine annealing to 10 −6, weight decay 10 −4, batch size 64, gradient clipping at one, at most 150 epochs, and early-stopping patience of 20 epochs. The selected epoch minimizes Eq. (35) on the validation targets. 2. The selected development checkpoint and optimizer state are discarded. The model, including the vector

The rolling prior remains forecast-origin specific and is rebuilt from its trailing information window. 3. The refitted model is evaluated once on the final 30%. The default seeds are {1, 2, 3, 4, 5}. Python, NumPy, and PyTorch seeds are set jointly; deterministic PyTorch/CuDNN behavior is requested where available. For reported ensemble forecasts, predictions are averaged across seeds before MSE, MAE, DM, or MCS calculations. 

4.3 Models for Assessing Incremental Accuracy 

To systematically isolate the empirical sources of forecast improvements, our external benchmark suite is organized hierarchically by the specific econometric restriction it relaxes. The node-specific univariate HAR model serves as the foundational anchor, addressing the primary baseline question of whether foreign-market information contains any incremental predictive value. Multivariate linear extensions—namely the unrestricted Vector HAR (VHAR) and the HAR-Kitchen Sink (HAR-KS) model, which linearly incorporates the latest active daily realization from all foreign markets—evaluate whether a linear cross-market expansion is sufficient. The Graph Neural Network (GNN-HAR) and Diffusion Convolutional Recurrent Neural Network (DCRNN-HAR), both estimated using pre-test static adjacency matrices, assess the contribution of non-linear spatial graph convolutions and recurrent propagations. Finally, the Pure-ST-Transformer incorporates temporal and data-driven spatial self-attention while deliberately omitting both the econometric prior and the frozen HAR anchor, serving as an unconstrained benchmark to evaluate flexible machine learning in the absence of economic regularization. All models are estimated independently at each forecast horizon (h ∈ { 1, 5, 22 }) under identical sample partitions and random seeds. To complement the external benchmark comparisons, we construct a nested structural ablation hierarchy designed to decompose the internal mechanics of the forecasting system: 1. HAR : The deterministic, horizon-specific linear HAR anchor without neural corrections. 2. HAR+Temporal-MHSA : The HAR anchor augmented with masked temporal multi-head self-attention, omitting the spatial channel. 3. HAR+Dynamic-Prior-Graph : The HAR anchor augmented with spatial aggregation restricted exclusively to the rolling GFEVD econometric prior. 4. HAR+MHSA+Dynamic-Graph : Combines temporal self-attention with prior-only spatial propagation, corresponding to setting g = 0 in the hybrid spatial mixture. 5. PGA-Scalar-g: The complete two-channel spatio-temporal architecture with a fixed, non-trainable scalar mixture parameter g = 0 .5 shared across all markets, calendar positions, and blocks. 6. PGA-Trans-HAR : The complete proposed framework, which replaces the fixed scalar with a trainable, node-specific gating vector gn = σ(γn), thereby capturing persistent cross-market heterogeneity in prior reliance while retaining temporal stability. ## 5 Empirical Results 

The empirical investigation is structured around two central econometric questions: first, whether incorporating strictly forecast-origin-admissible cross-market information provides measurable predictive gains over the univariate HAR baseline; and second, whether the structural regularization and gating restrictions imposed on that information are necessary to secure those gains. Throughout this section, all reported deep learning forecasts represent prespecified five-seed ensembles, with point predictions averaged across random seeds prior to the calculation of loss metrics and statistical tests. Our baseline evaluations are conducted on the full union-calendar panel containing all active target dates, while the common-trading-day subpanel provides an explicit calendar-robustness benchmark. The empirical evidence is organized sequentially: we first evaluate point forecast accuracy against HAR and the broader suite of external models, establish statistical significance through Diebold–Mariano and Model Confidence Set procedures, and finally evaluate internal component contributions via nested structural ablations. Prior to evaluating forecast errors, Figure 4 characterizes the empirical properties of the origin-admissible econometric information supplied to the hybrid spatial attention mechanism. The displayed matrix represents the arithmetic average across the unique row-normalized GFEVD prior matrices generated for the h = 1 out-of-sample forecast origins, assigning equal weight to each distinct 20-origin graph refresh rather than mechanically overweighting matrices that remain active across multiple consecutive trading days. The estimated diagonal shares range from 0.278 for France (FCHI) to 0.492 for Hong Kong (HSI), establishing that strong domestic persistence coexists with substantial foreign-market predictive content across the panel. Regionally, the estimated prior reveals dense intra-European integration, with mutual spillover shares reaching 0.216 from Germany (GDAXI) to FCHI and 0.202 from FCHI to GDAXI. Furthermore, the US market (SPX) exhibits pervasive outward predictive transmission, accounting for notable variance shares in the UK (FTSE, 0.173), Japan (N225, 0.175), and continental European markets. In contrast, Asia-Pacific indices generally retain higher own-market variance shares, though N225 continues to receive nontrivial predictive weight from the US. ## 6 Discussion 

The empirical evidence is most naturally interpreted as a result about the value of constrained information rather than the superiority of model complexity. PGA-Trans-HAR delivers its most consistent predictive gains at the weekly and monthly horizons, whereas daily rankings remain more heterogeneous across loss functions and markets. Furthermore, the structural ablations demonstrate that neither temporal self-attention, a rolling connectedness prior, nor 19 Forecasting Global Volatility Across Asynchronous Markets: Incremental Accuracy from Constrained Cross-Market Attention A P REPRINT 

an unconstrained parameter allocation is sufficient on its own to uniformly improve volatility forecasts. Consequently, the relevant economic insight is the disciplined, regularized utilization of foreign-market predictors relative to the HAR benchmark, rather than the isolated presence of a deep neural network. This section discusses that methodological interpretation and outlines its theoretical and empirical boundaries. 

6.1 The Value of Parsimony with Bounded Corrections 

The rolling econometric prior and data-driven spatial self-attention encode distinct and complementary forms of predictive information. Spatial self-attention is inherently flexible: conditional on the learned latent representations, it dynamically allocates weights across source markets and observation windows without imposing functional restrictions. This flexibility is valuable when cross-market predictive linkages exhibit complex non-linearities or regime shifts that are inadequately captured by a linear VAR. However, this unconstrained parameterization substantially expands the effective hypothesis search space, making the estimated attention weights sensitive to finite-sample transactional noise. This vulnerability is especially acute in the present empirical setting, where the cross-section contains only eight markets, volatility is persistent and strongly comoving, and the historical sample includes infrequent but extreme crisis episodes. The ridge-VAR/GFEVD channel addresses this limitation by supplying a structured, low-variance reference point. Ridge regularization stabilizes autoregressive coefficient estimates in the presence of highly collinear, persistent predictors by trading a modest amount of shrinkage bias for a substantial reduction in estimation variance (Hoerl and Kennard, 1970). The generalized forecast-error variance decomposition then converts the fitted VAR into directional predictive shares invariant to the Cholesky ordering (Pesaran and Shin, 1998; Diebold and Yilmaz, 2012). In our framework, this prior is dynamically re-estimated every 20 forecast origins strictly from a trailing historical window of up to 252 union trading days. It is therefore a strictly origin-admissible, reduced-form forecasting prior rather than a static graphical topology or a maintained structural truth. The market-specific gating vector formalizes the core bias–variance trade-off between flexible attention and econometric shrinkage. Equations (18) and (20) define the exact convex allocation implemented in the system. These empirical estimates indicate that the fitted system retains a dominant reliance on the structured econometric prior across all horizons, while assigning a modestly larger allocation to data-driven attention as the forecast horizon extends. These gating estimates must be interpreted in conjunction with the structural ablation findings. The rolling econometric prior alone does not uniformly outperform HAR, and an ungated, mechanical combination of temporal attention and the prior graph deteriorates forecast accuracy at longer horizons. Conversely, the gated convex mixture becomes highly effective at h = 5 and h = 22 : replacing a fixed scalar gate ( g = 0 .5) with learned market-specific gates ( gn) delivers uniform MSE and MAE reductions across all eight markets at weekly and monthly horizons, but not at the daily horizon. These findings confirm that information channels act as true complements only after their relative contributions are structurally regularized. They reject the unconstrained proposition that greater neural flexibility is monotonically beneficial; at the daily horizon, the stronger shrinkage imposed by a uniform scalar gate provides an indispensable safeguard against estimation variance. 

HAR anchoring and bounded corrections. The classical HAR model constitutes a formidable benchmark because its cascade of daily, weekly, and monthly averages provides a remarkably parsimonious approximation of long-memory volatility persistence (Corsi, 2009). Rather than forcing the neural network to reconstruct the full conditional volatility distribution from scratch, the proposed framework estimates a direct linear HAR model first and freezes its parameters. Equation (30) then injects the bounded neural correction αnrb,n δb,n into the HAR forecast within the inverse-softplus domain. Because αn is initialized at zero, optimization begins exactly at the linear HAR baseline. Volatility non-negativity is strictly enforced by the outer softplus transformation, while the tanh residual activation, the sigmoid gate 

rb,n , and the parameter bound αn strictly limit the amplitude of the latent adjustment. This architectural anchoring has two critical econometric implications: 1. Search Space Reduction: It transforms the learning objective from estimating the unconstrained volatility level to estimating a tightly restricted local deviation around a strong econometric baseline. This structural 20 Forecasting Global Volatility Across Asynchronous Markets: Incremental Accuracy from Constrained Cross-Market Attention A P REPRINT 

restriction shrinks the effective function space, accelerates convergence, and insulates early training from explosive or numerically unstable predictions. 2. Explicit Baseline Fallback: It preserves a deterministic econometric fallback. If the neural representation contains negligible incremental predictive content for a specific market or date, the residual gate and amplitude parameter shrink the adjustment, keeping the final forecast closely tethered to HAR. Concurrently, the node-standardized base MSE and the smooth-worst relative-regret penalty actively prevent the optimizer from purchasing aggregate panel improvements by accepting severe forecast degradation in smaller or peripheral markets. While the observed forecasting stability is fully consistent with this design, it should not be interpreted as an absolute mathematical proof that inverse-softplus anchoring unconditionally guarantees superior convergence in all settings. Establishing such a universal claim would require exhaustive comparisons of loss landscapes, optimization trajectories, and initialization sensitivities across otherwise identical architectures. Furthermore, this structural anchoring involves an explicit trade-off: when the underlying linear HAR baseline is persistently misspecified, the bounded nature of the neural residual may restrict the model from adapting rapidly to permanent structural shifts in the conditional mean. The HAR anchor is therefore best understood as a deliberate bias–variance choice that trades unconstrained end-to-end flexibility for guaranteed non-negativity, benchmark transparency, and bounded departures from established volatility dynamics. The empirical comparison with the Pure-ST-Transformer strongly supports this bias–variance interpretation. The unconstrained Transformer incurs higher average forecast losses than the simple HAR baseline at daily and weekly horizons, yet becomes highly competitive at the monthly horizon and minimizes losses for several individual markets. Consequently, the empirical evidence demonstrates that parsimony paired with bounded economic corrections is essential in the short- and medium-term settings where the proposed system performs best, while confirming that anchoring does not universally dominate unconstrained estimation across every asset or horizon. 

6.2 Horizon-Dependent Value of Cross-Market Information 

The incremental predictive value of cross-market information varies systematically with the forecast horizon. Relative to the univariate HAR baseline, PGA-Trans-HAR reduces both MSE and MAE across all eight markets at the daily and weekly horizons ( h = 1 and h = 5 ), and across seven of the eight markets at the monthly horizon ( h = 22 ). In pairwise Diebold–Mariano tests against external benchmarks, favorable rejections under absolute loss are most concentrated at h = 5 (27 out of 48 comparisons), followed closely by h = 1 (23 comparisons), before moderating to 16 at h = 22 . Nonetheless, the monthly specification retains the lowest cross-market panel averages under both loss criteria and achieves full retention in the 90% Model Confidence Set for all eight markets. Consequently, the long-horizon performance is more accurately described as exhibiting reduced statistical discriminability—driven naturally by the expanding sampling variance of multi-step forecast errors—rather than an absence of incremental cross-market information. A plausible economic mechanism underlying this horizon pattern centers on the degree of information overlap between global spillovers and domestic persistence. At short and medium horizons ( h = 1 and h = 5 ), foreign-market signals provide fresh, contemporaneous transmission of external shocks that local historical lags have not yet assimilated. At the monthly horizon ( h = 22 ), however, low-frequency cross-market comovement overlaps substantially with the long-memory persistence components already effectively captured by the domestic HAR cascade (particularly its 22-day trailing average). As a result, the marginal predictive content of external signals becomes partially redundant relative to the strong domestic anchor. 

6.3 Limits of the Information Set 

The learned gate gn functions as a structural regularization parameter that dictates how predictive capacity is allocated between two normalized spatial information channels; it does not constitute an endogenous crisis indicator. Within any fitted horizon–seed specification, gn is held invariant across out-of-sample evaluation dates, precluding it from identifying high-frequency shifts in market regimes around specific macroeconomic stress events. Furthermore, its numerical magnitude is conditioned on the relative scaling of the two attention sublayers, the row-normalization of the GFEVD prior, the loss function, the random initialization, and the downstream feed-forward network. Consequently, an estimated weight below 0.5 indicates only that the optimization pipeline places greater convex shrinkage on the econometric prior under this specific architectural parameterization. More broadly, empirical research demonstrates that learned attention weights do not necessarily provide faithful causal explanations of model predictions (Jain and Wallace, 2019). Cross-market variations in gn must therefore be interpreted as descriptive features governing predictive 21 Forecasting Global Volatility Across Asynchronous Markets: Incremental Accuracy from Constrained Cross-Market Attention A P REPRINT 

capacity allocation across the global panel, rather than as evidence that a specific market is intrinsically more “causal” or crisis-sensitive than another. 

The rolling prior. The GFEVD graph measures directional predictive connectedness within a reduced-form, ridge-regularized VAR system (Diebold and Yilmaz, 2012). Estimating this graph strictly from historical observations available at the forecast origin eliminates a critical source of look-ahead bias; it does not, however, satisfy the formal interventionist identification conditions required for structural causal inference (Pearl, 2009). Contemporaneously correlated structural innovations, unobserved global risk factors, market microstructure frictions, and asynchronous timezone closing orders can all influence the estimated forecast-error variance shares. Terminology such as “source,” “receiver,” and “transmission” in this paper consequently refers strictly to the directional decomposition of predictive variance within an estimated linear system, rather than to an identified structural economic transmission mechanism. 

The asynchronous-calendar mask. The asymmetric masking mechanism is formulated to resolve a precise data-admissibility challenge. By mathematically zeroing out inactive markets as temporal and spatial key/value sources, the network prevents zero-coded holiday closures from being misinterpreted as observed states of near-zero volatility. Concurrently, allowing a closed market to remain active as a query enables it to receive contemporaneous state representations from exchanges that are open on that union-calendar date. While this asymmetric routing is appropriate for preserving the flow of predictive information across a misaligned panel, it does not construct a structural causal model of international financial integration. In particular, daily union-calendar alignment cannot capture intraday closing sequences, non-overlapping trading hours, overnight information accumulation, or the institutional mechanisms governing market closures. The mask must therefore be understood as an information-admissibility device that respects out-of-sample filtration boundaries, rather than as a causal identification strategy. 

Further limitations and extensions. Several methodological limitations remain that provide fruitful avenues for future research: First, the predictive information set is intentionally parsimonious. The current framework does not incorporate scheduled macroeconomic announcements, global sentiment gauges, option-implied volatility surfaces, realized returns, market liquidity proxies, or foreign exchange fluctuations as conditioning variables. Macroeconomic news releases are known to play a dominant role in international price discovery (Andersen et al., 2007), while implied volatility measures contain valuable forward-looking information regarding future jump dynamics and continuous volatility (Busch et al., 2011). Incorporating these multimodal predictors could help disentangle common global macroeconomic shocks from genuine market-to-market predictive linkages. Any such extension must rigorously maintain release-time alignment: macroeconomic data revisions or foreign variables realized after a domestic market close cannot be treated as part of the information set available at the forecast origin. Second, the market-specific gate intentionally eliminates time-series flexibility within the test partition. While this parameterization is strongly supported by our ablation evidence—particularly at the daily horizon, where unconstrained flexibility inflates parameter estimation variance—it prevents the convex mixture from adapting to structural shifts that occur entirely within the out-of-sample period. A promising extension would involve a slowly evolving or hierarchical shrinkage gate that updates strictly in origin time. Our empirical finding that unconstrained time-varying gates deteriorate forecasting accuracy demonstrates that such flexibility must be accompanied by explicit economic shrinkage and validated out-of-sample, rather than assumed to be beneficial a priori .Third, the rolling econometric prior relies on specific design choices: a 252-day estimation window, a 20-origin update interval, a VAR lag order of p = 2 , a fixed ridge penalty, and a 22-step GFEVD horizon. This configuration is held constant across all forecast horizons to maintain a unified origin-time protocol; we do not claim that it is globally optimal. Systematic sensitivity analyses across alternative estimation windows and shrinkage intensities, conducted strictly within in-sample validation partitions, would clarify how prior configuration choices interact with forecast accuracy. A related structural limitation is that separate direct models are estimated for h = 1 , 5, and 22 ; their forecasts are not constrained to satisfy temporal aggregation or cross-horizon coherence properties. Fourth, the current empirical analysis focuses exclusively on conditional point forecasts. A point forecast is fundamen-tally tied to the mathematical functional elicited by its specific loss metric, and point accuracy alone cannot characterize tail risk or full distributional uncertainty (Gneiting, 2011). Extending the framework to generate predictive intervals or conditional density forecasts would facilitate formal density evaluation and calibration diagnostics. For Basel-compliant risk management, the joint modeling of Value-at-Risk (VaR) and Expected Shortfall (ES) is particularly relevant, as these risk measures admit joint elicitability under consistent scoring functions despite ES lacking elicitability in isolation (Fissler and Ziegel, 2016). Implementing such capabilities requires a quantile-based or distributional output layer and appropriate scoring rules; it cannot be directly extrapolated from point-loss MSE and MAE results. 22 Forecasting Global Volatility Across Asynchronous Markets: Incremental Accuracy from Constrained Cross-Market Attention A P REPRINT 

Finally, the empirical panel is confined to eight developed and emerging equity indices observed over the 2006–2022 period. Although this sample spans multiple severe systemic crisis episodes and evaluations are conducted across both all-days and common-days panels, the cross-sectional breadth and post-2022 regime coverage remain limited. Replicating the methodology across broader geographic regions, alternative asset classes (such as commodities or fixed income), and more recent post-pandemic data would provide valuable external validation. Furthermore, complementing statistical loss comparisons with economic-value assessments—such as volatility-timing investment strategies or risk-parity portfolio allocations accounting for turnover and transaction costs—would quantify the practical financial utility of the framework. In summary, while the proposed architecture establishes clear incremental predictive gains within this empirical setting (particularly at weekly and monthly horizons), the magnitude and statistical precision of that increment remain inherently dependent on the market, forecast horizon, and loss function. 

## 7 Conclusion 

Forecasting volatility across international equity markets is fundamentally an information-set problem. In a multi-market union calendar, an econometrically valid forecasting system must preserve informative signals from active exchanges, prevent holiday closures from being conflated with states of near-zero volatility, and strictly ensure that every predictive input terminates on or before the relevant forecast origin. This paper investigates whether foreign-market information, once subjected to these rigorous filtration boundaries, delivers statistically significant incremental forecast accuracy beyond the univariate HAR baseline and established multivariate volatility models. The proposed PGA-Trans-HAR framework operationalizes this inquiry through four interconnected structural restric-tions: 1. A rolling ridge-VAR/GFEVD prior that compresses cross-market predictive connectedness strictly using origin-admissible observations, updated on a 20-origin calendar. 2. A learned, market-specific gating vector that provides a parsimonious, date-invariant convex allocation between the structural econometric prior and data-driven spatial attention. 3. An asymmetric calendar mask that excludes inactive markets as information sources while allowing closed nodes to receive contemporaneous representations from active foreign exchanges. 4. A frozen, horizon-specific HAR baseline that anchors a bounded neural residual correction parameterized within the inverse-softplus domain. Consequently, the resulting framework is not intended as an unconstrained, black-box alternative to traditional econo-metrics; rather, it provides a disciplined, controlled testing procedure designed to isolate and quantify the marginal predictive contribution of cross-market information. The empirical evidence answers the central research question in a qualified affirmative. Relative to the univariate HAR benchmark, the constrained system reduces both MSE and MAE across all eight global markets at the daily and weekly horizons ( h = 1 and h = 5 ), and across seven of the eight markets at the monthly horizon ( h = 22 ). Relative to the broader suite of linear and deep learning benchmarks, the framework achieves the lowest cross-market average MAE at the daily horizon, and the lowest average MSE and MAE at the weekly and monthly horizons. Pairwise HAC-adjusted Diebold–Mariano tests confirm that these improvements are statistically significant in numerous comparisons—most prominently under daily and weekly absolute loss—while Model Confidence Sets indicate broader statistical competition across specifications as the forecast horizon extends. Furthermore, the structural ablation findings reject a complexity-driven explanation: temporal self-attention and the rolling prior graph are not unconditional complements, and the learned market-specific gate improves upon uniform scalar shrinkage at weekly and monthly horizons, but not at the daily horizon. The overarching methodological implication for financial forecasting is that, in environments characterized by low signal-to-noise ratios, architectural flexibility is most effective when its information filtration and adjustment capacity are governed by economically interpretable restrictions. The rolling prior, asymmetric mask, market-level allocation, and HAR anchor render the source of the predictive increment fully transparent, preventing the flexible neural component from unconstrainedly overfitting historical volatility levels. This evidence does not establish uniform model dominance, structural macroeconomic causality, or the universal superiority of deep neural networks. Instead, it demonstrates that the disciplined, bounded integration of classical econometric structure with data-driven attention represents a robust and viable path toward achieving genuine incremental forecast accuracy. Extending this framework to accommodate intraday timing sequences, richer multimodal macro-financial information sets, coherent cross-horizon density forecasting, and transaction-cost-adjusted portfolio decisions remains a promising agenda for future research. 23 Forecasting Global Volatility Across Asynchronous Markets: Incremental Accuracy from Constrained Cross-Market Attention A P REPRINT
